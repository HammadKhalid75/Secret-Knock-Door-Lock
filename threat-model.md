# Threat model - Secret Knock Door Lock

Our project protects access to the room or cupboard secured by the prototype lock, the belongings inside it, and the secrecy of the trained knock pattern. The most realistic threat is a curious visitor, classmate, or opportunistic person who wants to enter without permission by guessing the pattern, repeating knocking sounds they have heard, or causing the lock to open through background noise; we do not assume a highly skilled attacker or protection against physical destruction of the door. A proportionate response is to compare both knock count and relative timing, reject incorrect or incomplete patterns, test the device against other knocks and everyday sounds, keep the trained pattern and recordings local to the team and ESP32 rather than transmitting them, and automatically relock after a short unlock period. We will also avoid presenting the prototype as a high-security lock and retain a conventional access method for recovery. These measures are appropriate because they reduce casual misuse and accidental unlocking without adding disproportionate cost or complexity to a student prototype.
